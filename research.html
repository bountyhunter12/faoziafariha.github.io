Research

I want large language models to work well outside the settings they are usually built for: with limited compute and energy budgets, limited labelled data, and languages other than English.

Overview

Most of the field's progress assumes centralised data, abundant compute, and English text. My work asks what changes when those assumptions fail: when training has to happen across many low-resource clients rather than one large cluster, when the language in question is Bangla rather than English, and when a model's internal state, not just its output, needs to be defended.

My undergraduate thesis takes on the first problem directly, in federated fine-tuning. My shared-task work with the NLP Lab at CUET takes on the second, in multilingual and Bangla-language NLP. My current internship takes on the third, in the security of LLM-based agents. I see these as related questions about the same underlying goal: language models that are efficient, fair, and trustworthy outside their best-resourced use cases.

Research Threads

1. Green AI & federated learning

My undergraduate thesis, GreenFed-LoRA, designs a federated fine-tuning framework for large language models that treats energy consumption and carbon emissions as core optimisation objectives rather than side effects. I combine LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning with a federated architecture to reduce communication overhead and computational cost across heterogeneous clients, and evaluate the accuracy–energy trade-off against centralised and standard federated baselines on benchmark NLP tasks.

2. NLP for low-resource languages

With the NLP Lab, CUET, I have worked on shared tasks that push instruction-tuned LLMs (Qwen, LLaMA) into settings with little labelled data: few-shot extraction of psycholinguistic markers and conspiracy signals for SemEval-2026 Task 10, and multilingual joke generation under lexical and topical constraints with small instruction-tuned models for SemEval-2026 Task 1. Independently, I built an LLM-based reasoning pipeline for more than 1,500 Bengali physics multiple-choice questions, reaching 82% zero-shot accuracy with chain-of-thought prompting and no fine-tuning at all.

3. Multimodal AI & AI safety

For the LT-EDI 2025 Shared Task, I helped build a multimodal misogyny-detection system that fuses BERT-based text encoders with Vision Transformer image embeddings, placing 6th worldwide among all participating systems. I am now applying a similar attention to model internals in a different direction, at ELITE Research Lab, where I investigate AI agent security: designing threat models and mitigation strategies for persistent memory vulnerabilities in multi-agent LLM pipelines.
